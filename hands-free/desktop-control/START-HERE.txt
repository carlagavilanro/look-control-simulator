CURRENT VERSION 0.4.45 - READY-MADE SAMSUNG TV BUTTONS; RECORDING WAITS FOR A WHOLE SIGNAL
READY-MADE BUTTONS FOR A SAMSUNG TV
Samsung's smart remote sends infrared for its power key only. All its
other keys go to the TV by Bluetooth, which the receiver cannot hear,
so they cannot be recorded. The TV itself still takes infrared for
every function, with Samsung's standard codes.
  - In "Set up a device", choose the TV, then "Add a button", then
    "Samsung TV buttons". Eighteen buttons are added at once: On/off,
    Volume up and down, Mute, Channel up and down, Input, Home, Menu,
    the four arrows, OK, Back, Exit, Play and Pause.
  - On the website's Setup page the TV has a button "Add the 18
    Samsung TV buttons".
  - A button you recorded whole under one of those names stays as it
    is. One that was only part of a signal is replaced.
  - Try one. If the TV reacts, they all work. If it does not, the TV
    is not taking Samsung's standard codes, and the buttons can be
    removed again.

RECORDING WAITS FOR A WHOLE SIGNAL
When a button is being recorded and the receiver catches only part of
its signal, that part is no longer saved as the button. (Two were, on
the first TV remote: one with 58 timings and one with 9, where a whole
key has 67. The TV ignored both.)
  - The app and the website say "Only part of the signal was heard, so
    it was not saved. Press ... again", and go on waiting. Each time
    there are at least 45 more seconds to press.
  - The button is saved when a whole signal arrives.
  - "Record it again" no longer replaces a good recording with a part.
  - A part is told in two ways: fewer than 12 timings is never a key;
    and beside the device's other buttons, a signal made of the same
    pulses but shorter than their codes is a part of one.
  - Parts stay in the website's list "Recordings from the receiver".
If this message keeps coming, the receiver is not getting a clean
signal: check the sensor's three wires (signal on D15, power on 3V3,
ground on GND), hold the remote a hand's length away, and try the
sensor on the website's Boards and Wi-Fi page (step 5).

TESTED: with both board programs running on a PC against the website's
own code, and on the pages in a browser with stand-ins. The ready-made
codes were checked against the boards' own infrared library: it sends
exactly these timings and reads them back as the same Samsung codes.
NOT yet tested: on the real TV and the real receiver, and this
version's screens in the app on Windows.

PREVIOUS VERSION 0.4.38 - A REPAIR THAT DOES NOT CHECK OUT IS NOT KEPT
What prompted it: the two recordings of 21 Nov, 07:19 and 07:20, made with
the laptop's own camera (zoom at 100 this time): a calibration, five more
head positions, then "Refine accuracy" and its check.

DO THE SAVED HEAD POSITIONS HELP? Yes, clearly.
Each position was checked on seven dots. The same check frames, read
three ways (average, px):

    where the head was          calibration   that position's   the saved
    (against the calibration)   map alone     own map           positions
                                                                blended
    2 to one side, turned, tilted  115            85               85
    3 closer                       101            70               65
    4 tilted and nodded             75            63               60
    5 raised and nodded far        237            86               86
    6 to the other side, turned    103           111               90
    average                        126            83               77

  - The first check, right after the calibration, read 77 px.
  - Positions 5 and 6 did not pass their own checks (86 px with one dot
    391 px off; 111 px), so they are "test only" and are not used in
    normal use. They still carried information: on the last check of the
    session the saved positions with the learned head correction read
    75 px with all six and 105 px with the four that had passed.
  - Sitting back "the same" is not the same. At the last check the head
    was within what the app accepts as the calibration position (a
    little closer, nodded a little), and the calibration map alone read
    108 px there, about 74 px low. That is what the positions are for,
    and why a few around your usual one are worth it.
  - One map that uses head position, fitted to the calibration and all
    five positions together, did no better than the calibration map
    alone (118 px on average over the seven checks, against 117) and
    much worse than the separate maps. A map per position is right.
  - The head-movement step ran and was not used this time: the map with
    head position placed its own head-movement frames worse than the
    plain map did. That is the step working as meant.
  - With the camera's zoom at 100 each eye was 70 pixels wide and the
    first check read 77 px; at 180 it was 105 pixels and 73 px. No real
    difference, and at 100 the face has room on every side.

DID THE REFINEMENT HELP? No, and it did harm.
  - Its nine dots showed no place that read differently from its
    neighbours. They showed that everything read about 125 px low at
    that moment. It corrected that. Twenty seconds later, at its check,
    everything read about 74 px low, so the corrected map read 52 px
    high. Result: 103 px, where the map before it reads 108 px on the
    same frames. Nothing gained.
  - The check failed (it would have failed with either map), and the
    refined map, now marked "alignment needed", was saved over position 1:
    the calibration that had passed at 77 px.
  - What that means for what is saved now: position 1 carries a
    correction of 31 px left and 126 px up that was right for one moment.
    A new calibration is the clean way out (it starts the positions
    afresh). "Adjust saved alignment - 5 points", sitting as for the
    calibration, should rebuild that position and keep the others.

WHAT CHANGED
A refinement, a realignment or a one-point correction is now judged on
the frames of the check that follows it, against the map it started
from. It is not kept when it gains nothing there, or when it fails the
check, the map before it had passed, and it is not at least a fifth
better. The map from before then stays exactly as it was, with its own
check, and the bottom bar says so with both numbers. Adding a head
position is not affected: that makes a new map and replaces none.
Checked in a browser with a stand-in camera three ways: a refinement
that holds is kept; a refinement and a realignment that do not hold are
dropped and the saved calibration is unchanged.

NOT CHANGED, AND WHY
  - Positions that fail their check stay "test only". Letting them in
    would have helped here, but a failed check is the only sign the app
    has that a map is poor.
  - Choosing between the blend and the learned head correction. With
    three positions saved the learned correction read worse on the next
    check (104 px against 65); with six it read better (56 against 90,
    75 against 101). Four cases are too few to change the rule.

    eyes" uses. On 20 Nov it turned the camera's gain down from 100 to 22,

  - The wording no longer assumes glasses.

the eyelid input should stop being upset by one stalled dot. This could not

PREVIOUS VERSION 0.4.27 - A HEAD-MOVEMENT STEP, AND A MAP THAT CAN USE IT
What was asked: build the calibration step in which the head is moved on
purpose, and a map that uses it.

What the recordings showed first. The gaze is the eyes' turn in the head plus
the head's own turn. In every recorded session the head follows the dots:
it turns by about the same small amount per screen width in calibration and
in the checks. A map made from the eye readings alone cannot see the head's
share, so a look made with a little more or less head than usual lands off.

A map that reads the head as well (position, size, tilt, turn and nod, beside
the eye readings) was fitted to each recorded calibration and scored on the
checks recorded after it. Average error, map in use -> head-aware map:
    15 Nov, wide lens:       66 and 74 px  ->  61 and 57 px
    15 Nov, wide lens:       62 px         ->  53 px
    20 Nov, wide lens:       110 and 122   ->  66 and 85 px
    20 Nov, telephoto lens:  85 px         ->  67 px
Two cautions. Its settings were chosen on these same six checks, so this is
how well it can fit them, not a forecast. And a map fitted to still-head dots
alone learns the head's share from the way the head follows the dots: it
goes wrong if the head later stops following, or moves in a way calibration
never showed (leaning in, for example). That is what the new step is for.

The head-movement step. After the calibration dots, three more dots appear
in turn, about 7 seconds each. Keep looking at the dot and slowly move your
head: small circles with your nose, then tilt towards each shoulder and lean
a little closer and back. Small movements are enough. The gaze stays on the
dot, so everything that changes in the readings comes from the head.
  - If the head does not move during the first dot, the step ends by itself
    (about 20 seconds lost). The Skip head movement button ends it at any
    time.
  - It can be switched off: Setup, More setup options, Head-movement step.
  - It is left out in Eyes close-up tracking, which cannot see the head.

How the map is chosen afterwards. Three maps are compared on frames they
were not fitted to: the plain map, the head-aware map from the still dots
alone, and the head-aware map fitted with the head-movement frames as well.
  - With head-movement frames: it is used if it is at least 15% better than
    the plain map on head-movement frames and no worse on left-out dots.
  - From the still dots alone: it is used if it is at least 3% better on
    left-out dots, at most 15% worse on head-movement frames, and better on
    the two together.
  - If both qualify, the better one overall is taken. If neither does, or the
    eyes left the dot during the step, the plain map stays.
  - Without head-movement frames the plain map is always used, as before.
The status line after the check says whether head position is used, and if
not, why. A head reading is only used within the span the map was fitted on.

Saved diagnostics now hold the head-movement frames, the scores of the three
maps, and how far each eye is open in every frame (for later work on reading
up and down). Numbers only, as before.

How this was tested: unit tests with a made-up person whose head takes a
share of the gaze (head moving, head still, eyes leaving the dot, glances
away, saving and reading back); the six recorded checks above; the real page
in a headless browser with a stand-in tracker (head moves, head still, Skip,
step switched off, eyes wandering, and the 0.4.26 lens flow). No recording
with deliberate head movement exists yet, so what the step does for a real
person is not known. Please calibrate with the step and save diagnostics.

GETTING STARTED
1. Extract the entire ZIP. Keep the folders next to the executable.
2. Open Hands Free Screen Control.exe on a Windows 10/11 computer.
3. Enter your name. Your saved calibration still works with this update on the same camera and display. Choose a new accuracy check if you want to measure it again.
4. Detailed calibration is the default: 25 screen locations, with two extra visits to the center (27 dots total). Standard uses 13 locations plus two center revisits. Look at the dot until it moves. Blinking is okay.
5. Seven different dots then check accuracy. After a passing check, the controls open automatically. Look near the center of a large tile for TWO SECONDS to select it. Look away after selecting.
6. If the check fails, setup finishes with the measured errors. Adjust alignment collects five new reference points to correct a consistent offset or distortion, followed by seven fresh accuracy checks. You can also test the pointer without sending any clicks, or start a new calibration.

CALIBRATION FEATURES
The app compares combined-eye, separate-eye, and numerical models that learn head-motion correction within each fixed target using whole screen locations left out of training. A more complex map is selected only when it improves those results. Repeated center observations detect drift and are held out together during model comparison.

Calibration waits for settled eye measurements. The camera preview and tracking messages are hidden while collecting a dot so they do not distract from the target. A point that cannot settle pauses with an option to retry that point; the app does not automatically cycle through completed points.

Automatic camera mode requests 1080p and measures tracking speed before calibration. If it is too slow, it requests 720p before collecting points. Actual resolution depends on the camera. Balanced mode requests 720p directly. Camera resolution stays fixed during a calibration.

The result report separates mapping error, sample scatter, head-position rejection, and unavailable eye measurements. Error statistics include finite estimates that fall outside the screen or are rejected for head position; these are not hidden from the results.

The five-point alignment can adjust a saved map after a moderate position change. A larger change requires a new calibration. Alignment points never double as the later accuracy check. The pass criteria have not been relaxed.

SAVING AND PRIVACY
Camera images, raw eye samples, desktop snapshots, and typed text stay in memory on this PC. They are not uploaded to Railway.

Calibration saves numerical model coefficients, camera/display settings, accuracy measurements, and your local profile name. It does not save video, images, or raw eye samples. The fitted map is saved before its separate accuracy check; failing that check does not erase the map.

Use saved calibration resumes on the same camera and display. Check accuracy again after moving the camera or changing posture. Adjust saved alignment can correct a moderate offset. A changed camera, resolution, display size, or scaling requires calibration for that configuration. Small window-size fluctuations are ignored; substantial changes restart measurements for the new viewport.

LIMITS
This version controls the PRIMARY display. Additional monitors, Android, macOS, drag-and-drop, right/double click, and Windows secure/elevated screens are not implemented.

This is direct horizontal and vertical gaze mapping, not the appliance controller's left/right/center gestures. A normal webcam cannot guarantee individual-pixel gaze accuracy. The zoom controls make small targets easier to select. The dashed pointer outline is based on a previous measured error; it is not a guarantee.

Automated tests use simulated measurements and do not establish accuracy for a real person. The on-device check measures the current person, camera, and position. Pause and check alignment if the pointer drifts.

The executable is an unsigned development build. Follow your school's or organization's software policy if Windows blocks it. Do not disable Windows security protections.
